Illinois’ 7th Congressional District, explained

How midterm elections pick a district’s representative, how the 7th District has voted, and neutral profiles of the people who ran in 2026.

The 2026 result. Democrat La Shawn Ford won the seat on November 3, 2026, succeeding Danny Davis, who retired after nearly 30 years. The Associated Press’s count, as of November 17 (about 99% of votes counted), showed Ford with 89.4% and Republican Chad Koppie with 10.6%. Counts may change before certification.

How midterm elections work

A midterm falls halfway through a president’s four-year term. The whole U.S. House is on the ballot, along with about a third of the Senate and many state offices. Each of the 435 House districts elects one representative to a two-year term.

Districts

States are divided into districts of roughly equal population. Lines are redrawn after each census, so a district can change shape over time.

Primaries

Each party first picks a nominee. In Illinois the 2026 primary was March 17. The winner needs the most votes, not a majority, which is how Ford won with 24.1% in a field of 13.

General election

The party nominees, plus any independents, face off in November. The candidate with the most votes wins. Illinois has no runoff for U.S. House races.

Safe seats

Many districts lean heavily to one party, so the primary often decides the real contest. The 7th is rated Solid Democratic by nonpartisan forecasters.

Ready to vote in the next election? Check dates and registration with the Illinois State Board of Elections or your county clerk.

History of the 7th District

The 7th covers downtown Chicago’s Loop, much of the West Side, and near-west suburbs including Oak Park, Forest Park, Maywood and Broadview. Danny Davis represented it from 1997 until his retirement. 2026 was the first open-seat race since 1996, when Davis himself first won.

How voters have behaved

  • Cook’s Partisan Voter Index rated the district D+34 heading into 2026, the 7th most Democratic in the nation.
  • In the 2024 presidential race, Kamala Harris took 82% and Donald Trump 17% in the district.
  • The real competition has been in Democratic primaries. In 2022, Kina Collins nearly unseated Davis, 45.7% to 51.9%.

2026 candidates and their policies

These profiles describe what each candidate has said and done, drawn from public records, candidate surveys and news coverage. Policy summaries are the candidates’ own stated positions. Tap a name to expand.

Democrats

Melissa Conyears-ErvinChicago City Treasurer · 2nd place, 20.8%

Background

Chicago City Treasurer since 2019 and a state representative from 2017 to 2019. Worked in the insurance industry before public office. Also ran in 2024, finishing second to Davis with 21.3%.

Stated priorities

  • Economy: homeownership, small business support, apprenticeships, fair wages and local manufacturing; backs a federal wealth tax on billionaires.
  • Costs: higher wages, lower health care and college costs.
  • Public safety: violence-prevention programs, police-community partnerships with accountability, and gun safety laws.

Things to know

  • An AIPAC-linked super PAC, the United Democracy Project, ran ads supporting her. Austin Weekly News reported a $2.8 million commitment.
  • In 2024 the Chicago Board of Ethics fined her $70,000 for violations of the city ethics ordinance.
  • Endorsed by City Clerk Anna Valencia and former Mayor Lori Lightfoot. Raised about $621,000 through February 25, 2026.
Anthony Driver Jr.Labor and civic organizer · 3rd place, 11.2%

Background

A Chicago organizer who describes himself as executive director of the SEIU State Council in Illinois and president of the city’s Community Commission for Public Safety and Accountability.

Stated priorities

  • Points to his role in Chicago’s $15 minimum wage and its first civilian police oversight board.
  • Pitches himself as a next-generation leader who can re-engage working-class and young voters.

Things to know

Raised about $224,000 through February 25, 2026. He placed third; Ford and Conyears-Ervin finished ahead of him.

Kina CollinsPolitical organizer · 4th place, 9.3%

Background

An organizer who has run in four straight Democratic primaries: 13.9% in 2020, 45.7% in 2022, 18.9% in 2024 and 9.3% in 2026.

Stated priorities

  • Medicare for All, housing as a human right, fair wages, and clean air and water.
  • Higher taxes on the wealthy and an increased federal minimum wage.
  • Public safety framed as public health: addressing gun violence, addiction and housing security.

Things to know

Jewish Insider described her as backed by Justice Democrats. Raised about $55,000 through February 25, 2026, far less than the top fundraisers.

Dr. Thomas FisherEmergency physician · 5th place, 7.5%

Background

A South Side-raised emergency physician who trained at Dartmouth, the University of Chicago and Harvard. He was a White House Fellow, a Cook County Medicaid leader, and wrote The Emergency, a book about a year in a Chicago ER.

Stated priorities

  • Universal health care.
  • Background checks and an assault weapons ban, plus community-based violence intervention.
  • Higher wages, union protections and lower housing and grocery costs.

Things to know

Endorsed by 314 Action. Raised about $800,000 and spent about $539,000 through February 25, 2026.

Jason FriedmanReal estate business owner · 6th place, 7.4%

Background

Owner and former president of a real estate business. Earlier worked in the office of Sen. Dick Durbin. Jewish Insider noted he sits on the Jewish United Fund board.

Stated priorities

  • Jobs through trade schools and unions.
  • Full funding for schools and teachers.
  • Reducing gun violence while addressing root causes of crime.

Things to know

Endorsed by the Joint Action Committee for Political Affairs. He was the top fundraiser in the field: about $2.5 million raised and $2.1 million spent through February 25, 2026, yet he finished sixth.

Republicans

Chad Koppie2026 Republican nominee · 10.6% in the general

Background

A Kane County farmer, Army veteran and retired Delta Air Lines captain. A 2024 profile said he has served on the Kane County Regional Board of School Trustees since 2007. He is the district’s repeat Republican candidate: a write-in in 2022, the nominee with 16.7% in 2024, and the nominee again in 2026 after winning the primary 65.8% to 34.2%.

Stated priorities

In his WTTW voter guide answer, he said his main goal is a constitutional amendment stating that each human life begins at conception.

Things to know

No fundraising was reported for him in the primary race, and he has not won more than about 17% in a general election in the district.

Patricia “P Rae” EasleyRadio host and educator · lost 2026 primary, 34.2%

Background

A Chicago native with a master’s in education from UIC, described as an educator, financial professional and media personality. She was an alternate delegate to the 2024 Republican National Convention from the 7th District.

Stated priorities

She said the district has been governed by career politics rather than measurable results, and at a candidate forum said she wants to bring manufacturing back to the district.

Things to know

She lost the Republican primary to Koppie, 1,682 votes to 3,240. No campaign fundraising was reported.
